Ancestral Landscapes

California is a mosaic of Indigenous stewardship. Long before European mapping, the land was a managed garden of oaks, rivers, and coastal sanctuaries. We recognize the Ohlone, Tongva, Pomo, and Chumash—not as figures of the past, but as living guardians of the future.

Cultural Burning

Fire is a gift. For generations, California tribes used intentional “Good Fire” to rejuvenate the land, clear invasive brush, and ensure the health of basket-weaving materials. Today, Traditional Ecological Knowledge (TEK) is essential in healing our wildfire-prone landscape.

Historical Timeline

Pre-Contact Era
Sophisticated ecological management, inter-tribal trade networks, and vibrant ceremonial life.

1769–1840s
The Mission Period: Spanish colonization introduces forced relocation and labor systems.

1848–1870s
Gold Rush: A period of state-sanctioned violence and mass land seizure.

1900–Present
The Era of Revitalization: Sovereign rights, language nesting, and cultural return.
